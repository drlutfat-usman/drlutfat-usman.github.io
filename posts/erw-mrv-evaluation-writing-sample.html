Enhanced rock weathering (ERW) carbon removal claims rest on a chain of measurement and modelling that spans five distinct phases: rock application, initial weathering, field processes, watershed transport, and ocean storage. A credible MRV (measurement, reporting, verification) plan should be explicit about which of these phases are directly measured, which are modelled, and how uncertainty is carried through each transition. This is the same evaluative discipline I apply to any large, multi-site scientific data pipeline — whether transcriptomic or geochemical.

What I Look For, Phase by Phase

1. Rock application: Is rock mineralogy and elemental composition characterized directly (e.g. XRD) rather than assumed from generic literature values? This sets the theoretical upper bound on CO2 removal — an unreliable input here undermines everything downstream, the same way an uncalibrated reference genome undermines a transcriptomic pipeline.

2. Initial weathering: Does the plan rely on multiple independent measurements (cation release, dissolved inorganic carbon, total alkalinity) to cross-validate the weathering signal, rather than a single proxy? Redundant, independently validated measurements are standard practice in any rigorous quantitative science — the same logic I apply when validating a differential expression result against multiple statistical methods.

3. Field processes: Are soil pH, cation exchange capacity, and greenhouse gas fluxes tracked over time, not just at a single endpoint? Weathering is a transient, ongoing process — this is a longitudinal data problem, not a single-timepoint measurement problem, and should be treated with the same time-series rigor as any repeated-measures biological dataset.

4. Watershed transport and ocean storage: These phases are, by necessity, modelled rather than directly measured at project scale. The key question is whether the model has been validated against independent empirical data, and whether the plan is transparent that this is a modelled estimate carrying additional uncertainty — not presented with the same confidence as a direct field measurement.

General MRV Rigor Criteria

• Baseline and additionality: is there a genuine pre-treatment control, not just a modelled counterfactual?

• Conservative crediting: when measurements fall within an uncertainty range, does the plan credit the lower bound?

• Data pipeline integrity: standardized sampling protocols, version-controlled data handling, and a documented chain of custody from quarry to field to lab.

• Longitudinal design: credit issuance tracks accumulating multi-year data rather than being front-loaded on Year 1 projections.

This is the same standard I apply when reviewing large international research proposals — checking that scientific claims are backed by named, verifiable data rather than asserted in prose.
